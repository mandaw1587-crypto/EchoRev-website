**Why this isn't $197 for everything:** $197 is priced as a low-friction entry point (already validated by three testimonials describing outcomes worth 10–20x that price — automated onboarding, page-one SEO in six weeks, tripled engagement). That gap between what $197 delivers and what it's priced at is not a pricing win, it's evidence you are dramatically underpricing the actual implementation work. The $197 session should be re-scoped internally as a paid discovery/quick-win call that qualifies buyers for the real offer — not the whole engagement. If Manny is currently doing full implementation work for $197, that is the single biggest margin leak in this business and should stop.

**Expected impact:** Moving the backend offer from an undefined "[price]" (currently defaulting to whatever gets negotiated on the spot, likely under $500 given how it's anchored against a $197 front-end) to a structured $3,500 core price with a $997/mo retainer option should lift revenue per client 7–15x versus treating $197 as the ceiling, while the 2–3 concurrent client cap actually gets *more* defensible, not less, at a higher price point.

**Key risk:** A solo operator claiming genuine white-glove scarcity ("2-3 people at a time") while charging $197 is incoherent — that price signals commodity, not scarcity. The price has to match the story already being told in the script, or prospects will subconsciously discount the scarcity claim as sales theater.

## Cost Analysis (Solo Operator Model)

Manny has no COGS in the traditional sense — his only real input is time, and time is the binding constraint the whole pricing model has to be built around.

**Fully-loaded cost driver: hours, not dollars.**

- Stated capacity: 2–3 clients "at a time" (per the close script) — this is a genuine constraint, not manufactured scarcity, because implementation work (building automations, SEO setup, content pipelines, web builds) requires hands-on, synchronous time that doesn't scale without hiring.
- Assume a Sprint (Tier 1) requires ~25–35 hours of Manny's direct time across a 3–4 week window (build + calls + iteration).
- Assume a Retainer (Tier 2) client requires ~4–6 hours/month once the initial build is live (monitoring, tweaks, new automations) — materially lower marginal cost than a fresh Sprint.
- **This is the critical insight for the pricing model:** the "2-3 at a time" scarcity constraint should be read as 2–3 concurrent *Sprint* slots (the hands-on build phase), not a cap on total clients. Retainer clients, once onboarded, sit in a lower-touch bucket and don't compete for the same scarce hours. This lets Manny stack recurring revenue on top of the capacity-constrained front end instead of being capped by it.

**Break-even / floor price:** If Manny values his own time at even a conservative $75/hr (well below what a "$25K+/year value delivered" consultant should charge), a 30-hour Sprint has an implicit labor cost of ~$2,250. Pricing the Sprint at $3,500 yields roughly $1,250 of margin above that floor before accounting for tools, software subscriptions, and customer acquisition cost (ads, content, outreach). **Anything priced at or near $197 for real implementation work is pricing below the labor floor** — it's not a discount, it's a loss.

**Cost reduction / leverage opportunities:**
- Standardize the automation workflows built for testimonial clients (onboarding automation, content pipeline, SEO checklist) into reusable templates — this reduces build hours per client over time and improves margin without cutting price.
- Use the Tier 0 $197 call to pre-diagnose which of the 4 pillars (automation, SEO, social, web) matters most, so Sprint time isn't wasted building things the client doesn't need.

## Market Context

- **Direct competitors** (freelance AI/automation consultants, small automation agencies): typical one-time implementation projects run $1,500–$8,000 depending on scope; monthly managed-automation retainers commonly run $500–$2,500/month.
- **Indirect competitors / substitutes:** DIY with ChatGPT/Zapier templates ($0–$50/month in tools + the client's own time), larger agencies ($5,000–$20,000+ builds, slower, less personal), doing nothing (status quo — the real competitor for a hesitant prospect).
- **Positioning:** The scarcity language ("2-3 people at a time," "I typically work with... so I can actually deliver results") plus the testimonial-driven authority ("page one in 6 weeks," "engagement tripled") supports a **premium/high-touch position**, not a competitive/race-to-the-bottom position. At $197, Manny is pricing like the DIY substitute, not like the differentiated alternative to a $5,000+ agency. That's the core mismatch this strategy fixes.
- **Price sensitivity:** Likely medium — the target buyer (freelancers, local business owners, content creators, per the testimonials) is price-aware but is buying back *time and stress*, which is a high-emotion purchase. This supports value-based anchoring over cost-plus.

## Value-Based Pricing Model

**Value metric:** hours saved per week, converted to dollars, over a 12-month horizon — this is *exactly* the frame the "too expensive" objection handler already uses ("what's that worth to your business over the next 12 months?"), so the pricing model should be built to answer that question with a real number instead of leaving it rhetorical.

```
PRICE = (Customer's Economic Value) × (Value Capture Ratio)

Time-savings value:
  10 hrs/week saved × 4.33 weeks/month × $50–$100/hr (blended value
  of a freelancer/small-biz owner's time, per testimonial segment)
  = $2,165 – $4,330 / month
  = $25,980 – $51,960 / year

Plus non-time value (not double-counted, treated as upside):
  SEO → page-one ranking → new inbound leads
  Social → consistent posting → audience growth → more leads
  Web → higher conversion rate on existing traffic
  (Conservatively worth another $500–$2,000/month per testimonial evidence)

Total 12-month value created: roughly $25,000 – $55,000 (time savings alone,
conservative end), climbing higher once lead-gen upside is counted.

Value Capture Ratio (competitive market, differentiated by high-touch
1-on-1 delivery): 10-20%

Recommended price = 10-15% of conservative annual value
  = $2,600 - $8,250 → supports the $3,500 core Sprint price
    comfortably within the defensible range.
```

This is also the exact number Manny should be pre-loading into the discovery call **before** he ever quotes a price, so that by the time he says "the investment is $3,500," the prospect has already done the math themselves and landed on a bigger number.

## Recommended Pricing Model & Packaging

**Model: Hybrid — one-time Sprint (value-based) + optional monthly Retainer (tiered/usage-adjacent, positioned as an add-on, not a requirement).**

Why hybrid over pure one-time or pure retainer:
- **Pure one-time** caps lifetime value per client and forces Manny to constantly refill the top of funnel to hit revenue targets — dangerous when he's also capacity-constrained to 2-3 concurrent builds.
- **Pure retainer** undersells the up-front implementation effort (which is genuinely front-loaded, high-effort work) and risks anchoring the relationship at too low a perceived value from day one.
- **Hybrid** lets the Sprint capture value from the intensive build phase at a premium price, while the Retainer captures ongoing value from lower-effort maintenance — matching price to actual cost-to-serve at each phase, and converting scarce Sprint slots into a compounding recurring-revenue base instead of a one-and-done transaction.

**Packaging:**

| Package | What's included | Price | Positioning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diagnostic Call | 1-on-1 session, pick highest-leverage pillar, quick win delivered live | $197 | Entry point — unchanged |
| **AI Implementation Sprint** | Full build: automation workflow(s) + one growth pillar (SEO or social or web) implemented, documented, handed off | **$3,500** | Core / most people land here |
| Full Syndicate Build | Automation + all three growth pillars (SEO + social + web) in one sprint | $4,500 | For prospects who want everything at once |
| Syndicate Partner (retainer) | Monthly monitoring, iteration, new automations, ongoing content/SEO management | $997/mo, 3-mo minimum | Attach after any Sprint tier — this is where LTV is built |

## Sensitivity Analysis — Core Offer (Tier 1 Sprint)

Base case assumes ~10 qualified sales calls/month reaching the close script stage (a reasonable volume for a solo operator running paid ads/content into a $197 front end).

| Price Point | Est. Win Rate | Monthly Deals (of 10 calls) | Monthly Revenue | Implied Margin* | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $2,800 (-20%) | ~65% | 6.5 | $18,200 | High volume, thin scarcity signal — undercuts the "2-3 at a time" premium story |
| $3,150 (-10%) | ~55% | 5.5 | $17,325 | Still leaves labor-floor margin, weaker anchor |
| **$3,500 (rec.)** | **~45%** | **4.5** | **$15,750** | Best balance of scarcity credibility + close rate; matches value-capture math |
| $3,850 (+10%) | ~35% | 3.5 | $13,475 | Fewer deals but each is more profitable; fits genuinely if capacity is the binding constraint anyway |
| $4,200 (+20%) | ~25% | 2.5 | $10,500 | Only worth it if paired with retainer attach rate pushing total contract value up |

*Margin assumes ~$2,250 labor-floor cost per Sprint at the $75/hr internal rate discussed above; margin per deal ranges from ~$550 (low end) to ~$1,950 (high end).

**Read on this table:** Because Manny is capacity-constrained to 2–3 concurrent Sprints regardless of price, the "more volume" scenarios at $2,800–$3,150 don't actually help him — he can't serve the extra demand anyway. **The capacity ceiling means he should price toward the top of this range, not the bottom.** If demand at $3,500 already exceeds 2-3 concurrent slots, that is the strongest possible signal to test $3,850–$4,200 next, not to discount.

**Retainer attach sensitivity:** Every 1 in 3 Sprint clients who attach the $997/mo retainer for even 3 months adds ~$2,991 in additional contract value with only ~15-18 extra hours of low-touch time across the quarter — this is the highest-leverage number in the whole model and should be the #1 thing tracked month over month.

## How the Capacity Constraint (2-3 clients) Should Shape Pricing

This is the single most important lever available to Manny, and the script is already halfway there — it just needs a price that matches the claim.

1. **Scarcity only works as a premium-pricing justification if the price itself feels scarce.** "I typically work with 2-3 people at a time" said right before quoting $197 reads as contradictory — genuine scarcity and commodity pricing don't coexist credibly. Said right before $3,500-$4,500, it reads as true premium positioning: *of course* he only takes 2-3 people, this is real work.
2. **Treat the 2-3 slot cap as a supply-side lever, not a volume lever.** Don't try to serve more people at a lower price to "make up" for low per-unit revenue — that's the exact anti-pattern of subsidized volume this framework warns against. Instead, raise price per slot until the win rate at that scarcity level feels earned rather than desperate (roughly the 35-45% win rate band above).
3. **Use the Retainer to decouple revenue growth from the Sprint capacity ceiling.** Since retainer clients need only ~4-6 hrs/month post-build, Manny can carry a growing base of retainer clients (10, 20, 30+ over time) alongside the fixed 2-3 concurrent Sprint slots without violating the "2-3 at a time" promise — because that promise is specifically about the intensive build phase, not the whole client relationship. This is how a capacity-constrained solo operator scales revenue without lying about the scarcity claim.
4. **Rotate the "open spot" language monthly**, not per-call. If every single call is told "one spot is open right now," it stops being scarcity and becomes a script tic. Keep a real, small waitlist (even 2-3 names) so the claim is verifiably true and Manny can reference it authentically: "I've actually got someone starting next week too, so this really would be the last spot for a few weeks."

## Presenting Price on the Existing Close Script

The close script in `sales-script.html` (Section 4) currently reads:

> "The investment is [price]. I typically work with 2–3 people at a time so I can actually deliver results. One of those spots is open right now."

**Recommended replacement (drop-in, same structure, same beats):**

> "Based on everything you've told me, here's what I'd do for you: [pillar-specific offer]. The investment is **$3,500** as a one-time build — no subscription required to get this live in your business. I typically work with 2-3 people at a time so I can actually deliver results, and one of those spots is open right now. If you want me to keep managing and improving it after we launch, there's an optional partner rate of $997 a month, but that's not required to get the full build. Does this feel like the right move for you?"

Why this specific phrasing:
- States the Sprint price as a **one-time investment** explicitly ("no subscription required") — this pre-empts a common unspoken objection (fear of getting locked into recurring billing) before it surfaces.
- Introduces the retainer as **optional and secondary**, so it doesn't inflate the perceived "ask" of the core decision — this keeps the primary close simple, which matters given the script's existing "wait, do not fill the silence" discipline.
- Keeps the scarcity line completely intact, since it's already well-built and shouldn't be touched.

## Anchor & Framing Techniques Tied to the Existing Objection Handlers

**1. Pre-load the "too expensive" handler with a self-generated anchor (before price is even quoted).**

The existing handler says: *"...what's that worth to your business over the next 12 months? If the number you're thinking is more than [your price]..."* — this only works if the prospect has a number in mind. Most won't unless asked earlier. Add one question to the Discovery Call Opener (Section 3), right after the pain amplification question:

> "Real quick — what would you say an hour of your time is worth to your business right now, roughly?"

Get their number (most will say $50-$150/hr). Do the math with them live: "So if we get you back even 10 hours a week, that's [their number x 10 x 4.33] a month, or [x12] over the year." Now, when the "too expensive" objection lands later and Manny asks "what's that worth over 12 months," the prospect is recalling a number *they stated themselves* earlier in the call — self-generated anchors are far stickier and harder to argue against than a number the seller supplies after the fact.

**2. Use decoy/relativity pricing to defuse "I need to think about it."**

The existing handler correctly digs for the real objection ("is it the investment, the timing, or something about whether this will work"). Pair this with sequencing on the close itself: **present the Full Syndicate Build ($4,500) or the Sprint+Partner bundle ($7,491 total) first**, then the Core Sprint ($3,500) as "or if you just want to start with the highest-leverage piece, that's $3,500." Anchoring high first makes $3,500 feel like the reasonable, lower-commitment option rather than the top-line price — which reduces the instinct to stall on "let me think about it" because the decision has already been reframed as "which size" rather than "yes or no."
